# General Integrated Pest & Disease Management (IPM) Principles

## Overview
IPM combines cultural, biological, and chemical methods, using chemical control as one tool among several rather than the default first response. This reduces cultivation cost, pesticide residue risk, and resistance development, while maintaining yield protection.

## Core principles
- Prevention first: resistant varieties, crop rotation, sanitation (removing infected debris), and balanced nutrition reduce disease/pest pressure before it starts.
- Regular scouting: early detection at low severity is far cheaper and more effective to manage than late-stage, high-severity outbreaks.
- Use economic thresholds: not every pest sighting justifies spraying — treatment should be proportionate to actual risk of economic loss.
- Rotate pesticide/fungicide mode-of-action groups across a season to slow resistance development in the pest/pathogen population.
- Always follow product label instructions exactly — dosage, timing, and pre-harvest interval — for safety and residue compliance; do not increase dose beyond label to compensate for perceived poor control, as this raises residue and worker-safety risk without improving efficacy and may indicate a different problem (resistance, misdiagnosis) that needs expert input instead.

## When confidence in a diagnosis is low
Do not apply broad-spectrum treatment speculatively. Refer to the local agriculture extension officer or a diagnostic lab for confirmation — misdiagnosis leading to the wrong treatment is a leading cause of wasted input cost and delayed real treatment.
