# Squash / Cucurbits — Powdery Mildew

## Overview
White, powdery fungal growth on upper and lower leaf surfaces, eventually causing leaf yellowing and dieback if severe. One of the most common cucurbit diseases.

## Favorable conditions
Unlike most fungal diseases, powdery mildew does not need free water on leaves — it thrives in moderate temperatures with high humidity but dry leaf surfaces, and can spread in dry conditions with high humidity.

## Integrated pest & disease management actions
- Choose resistant varieties where available — this is the most effective long-term control.
- Improve airflow through proper spacing and pruning of dense growth.
- Remove severely infected leaves to reduce spore load, but avoid removing so much foliage that fruit sunscald becomes a risk.

## Safe input use
Sulfur-based or other fungicides labeled for cucurbit powdery mildew can be used at first sign of infection; follow label rate, and note sulfur should not be applied in combination with or shortly after oil-based sprays or in very hot weather, as this can cause leaf burn — always check label compatibility notes.

## When to refer to an expert or lab
If mildew is spreading rapidly and covering the majority of the canopy despite treatment, consult extension for resistance-management guidance (fungicide rotation).

## Follow-up monitoring
Check twice weekly once first symptoms appear — this disease can progress quickly across a field.
