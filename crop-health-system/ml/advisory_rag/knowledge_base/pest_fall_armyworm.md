# Pest — Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda)

## Overview
A highly destructive caterpillar pest, especially on maize, that feeds on leaves (characteristic ragged "window-pane" feeding and frass in the whorl) and can bore into the growing point, causing severe yield loss if not caught early.

## Monitoring
Pheromone traps are the standard early-warning tool — a rising trap count over consecutive weeks is a strong leading indicator of an outbreak before visible crop damage appears. Scout the whorl of young plants regularly for frass and larvae.

## Integrated pest management actions
- Scout fields weekly, especially at the whorl stage of maize, for egg masses and young larvae — early-instar larvae are much easier to control than late-instar ones.
- Encourage natural enemies (parasitic wasps, predatory bugs) by avoiding unnecessary broad-spectrum insecticide use.
- Hand-removal of egg masses/larvae is effective and low-cost for small plots at low infestation levels.
- Intercropping and push-pull strategies (where locally validated) can reduce infestation pressure.

## Safe input use
If trap counts or scouting indicate an economic threshold has been crossed, use an insecticide registered for fall armyworm on your crop, applied while larvae are still small (early instar), and follow label dosage and pre-harvest interval. Rotate insecticide classes across the season to slow resistance development, which has already been documented in fall armyworm populations in several regions.

## When to refer to an expert or lab
Rapidly rising trap counts across a locality, or repeated crop damage despite treatment (a sign of possible resistance), should be reported to the extension office for area-wide coordination — fall armyworm outbreaks are often managed more effectively at a regional rather than single-farm level.

## Follow-up monitoring
Continue trap monitoring through the season; a single low count is not reassurance — track the trend over consecutive weeks.
