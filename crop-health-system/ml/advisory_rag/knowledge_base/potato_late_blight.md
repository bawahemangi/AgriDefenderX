# Potato — Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

## Overview
The same pathogen that causes tomato late blight. Water-soaked lesions on leaves and stems, often with white sporulation on leaf undersides in humid mornings; can rot tubers in storage if infected tubers are harvested.

## Favorable conditions
Cool, wet, humid weather (15-24°C) — classic monsoon or foggy-season risk period.

## Integrated pest & disease management actions
- Treat as a fast-emergency: destroy infected plants immediately, do not compost.
- Avoid irrigating foliage; improve drainage in low-lying, waterlogged areas of the field.
- Do not harvest from visibly infected plants for storage — infected tubers rot and spread the pathogen in storage.
- Coordinate with neighboring potato/tomato growers, since spores spread by wind and can cross farm boundaries quickly.

## Safe input use
Preventive fungicide programs timed to weather forecasts (before a cool, wet spell) are far more effective than reactive treatment once lesions appear. Use only products registered for potato late blight, follow label dosage and interval, and prioritize expert guidance given how quickly this disease can spread field-to-field.

## When to refer to an expert or lab
Escalate immediately — this is one of the highest-priority diseases for expert/lab confirmation and district-level alerting due to rapid spread potential.

## Follow-up monitoring
Daily checks during cool/wet weather windows; flag the case for hotspot-cluster monitoring so nearby farms are alerted.
