# Tomato — Leaf Mold (Passalora fulva)

## Overview
Pale yellow spots on the upper leaf surface with olive-green to grayish-purple velvety mold on the underside. Mostly a problem in humid, poorly ventilated conditions — common in polyhouse/greenhouse tomato and dense field plantings.

## Favorable conditions
High humidity (above 85%) with moderate temperatures; poor ventilation is the single biggest risk factor.

## Integrated pest & disease management actions
- Improve ventilation aggressively — this is the primary control lever, more important than spraying.
- Reduce plant density and prune lower leaves to increase airflow.
- Avoid overhead watering and reduce greenhouse/polyhouse humidity where possible (venting, wider row spacing).
- Remove and destroy infected leaves.

## Safe input use
Fungicide is a secondary measure here — ventilation/humidity control usually resolves mild cases. If needed, use a product labeled for tomato leaf mold at label rate, and prioritize resistant tomato varieties for future plantings in humid/greenhouse conditions.

## When to refer to an expert or lab
If symptoms persist despite ventilation improvements, or spread rapidly through a greenhouse, involve extension support to assess structural/ventilation causes.

## Follow-up monitoring
Check weekly, with extra attention after periods of closed/poor ventilation.
