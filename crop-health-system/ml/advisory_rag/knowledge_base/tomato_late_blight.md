# Tomato — Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans)

## Overview
A fast-moving, highly destructive oomycete disease. Causes water-soaked, dark green-to-brown lesions on leaves with a pale green-yellow halo, often with white fungal growth on the underside in humid conditions. Can destroy a field within days under favorable weather. Also affects potato and can spread between the two crops.

## Favorable conditions
Cool, wet weather (15-24°C) with high humidity or prolonged leaf wetness (fog, dew, drizzle). Spreads rapidly during monsoon-like conditions.

## Integrated pest & disease management actions
- This disease spreads extremely fast — act within 24-48 hours of first symptoms.
- Remove and destroy (do not compost) infected plants/plant parts immediately.
- Avoid overhead irrigation entirely during outbreak periods.
- Increase plant spacing and prune for airflow to reduce the humid microclimate the pathogen needs.
- Do not plant tomato near potato fields, especially if either had late blight in the previous season.
- Monitor weather forecasts — late blight risk spikes sharply after multiple consecutive cool, wet days.

## Safe input use
Late blight typically requires a protective fungicide program started *before* symptoms appear once weather conditions are favorable, since the pathogen spreads faster than reactive spraying can contain it. Use a product registered for late blight, follow label dosage and spray interval exactly, and rotate between fungicide mode-of-action groups to slow resistance development. Because of the speed and severity of this disease, this is a priority case for expert-guided spray decisions rather than self-directed treatment.

## When to refer to an expert or lab
Late blight should almost always be escalated to an extension officer immediately given how fast it spreads and its potential to wipe out a field and spread to neighboring farms. Treat any late-blight-consistent symptom during cool wet weather as a high-priority referral, even at low confidence.

## Follow-up monitoring
Daily visual checks during the outbreak window (cool + wet weather). Alert neighboring farms in the same hotspot cluster, since spores travel by wind and rain splash.
