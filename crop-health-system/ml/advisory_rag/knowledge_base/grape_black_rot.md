# Grape — Black Rot (Guignardia bidwellii)

## Overview
Reddish-brown circular leaf spots and, more damagingly, fruit that shrivels into hard black "mummies." Can cause major crop loss if uncontrolled during fruit development.

## Favorable conditions
Warm, humid weather during and after bloom; overwinters in mummified fruit and infected canes left in the vineyard.

## Integrated pest & disease management actions
- Remove and destroy mummified fruit and infected canes during dormant pruning — this drastically cuts the following season's infection source.
- Improve canopy airflow through canopy management/pruning.
- Remove wild/volunteer grapevines nearby that can harbor the pathogen.

## Safe input use
A protectant fungicide program from early shoot growth through several weeks post-bloom is standard in high-pressure regions; follow label rate and spray interval, since black rot fungicide programs are also timing-sensitive like apple scab.

## When to refer to an expert or lab
For vineyards with a history of black rot, involve extension support in building a season spray calendar, particularly around bloom, the most vulnerable period for fruit infection.

## Follow-up monitoring
Inspect fruit clusters weekly from bloom through veraison.
