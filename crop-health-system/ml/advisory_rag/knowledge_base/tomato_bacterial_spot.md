# Tomato — Bacterial Spot (Xanthomonas spp.)

## Overview
Small, dark, water-soaked spots on leaves, stems, and fruit, often with a yellow halo on leaves. Unlike fungal diseases, this is bacterial, so most fungicides are ineffective against it.

## Favorable conditions
Warm, wet, humid weather; spreads readily via splashing water, contaminated tools, and handling wet plants.

## Integrated pest & disease management actions
- Avoid working in the field when foliage is wet — this is the main way bacterial spot spreads between plants.
- Use disease-free/certified seed and transplants where possible; bacterial spot is often seed-borne.
- Remove and destroy infected plant debris after harvest; avoid overhead irrigation.
- Rotate out of tomato/pepper for at least 1-2 seasons in badly affected plots.

## Safe input use
Copper-based bactericides can reduce spread if applied early and preventively, but efficacy is limited once the disease is established, and copper resistance is increasingly reported — do not rely on spraying alone. Follow label rate and combine with the cultural practices above.

## When to refer to an expert or lab
Since bacterial and fungal spots can look similar to the naked eye but need different management, lab or expert confirmation is valuable, especially for first-time occurrence in a field.

## Follow-up monitoring
Check after every rain event; avoid field work during and right after rain while disease is active.
